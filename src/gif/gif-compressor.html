How it works

The GIF is decoded frame by frame in your browser (handling each frame's disposal method so the full picture is reconstructed correctly, not just the raw delta each frame stores on disk), then re-encoded with whichever levers you set: scaling every frame down to a percentage of the original size, keeping only every 2nd/3rd/4th frame, and/or a coarser color-quantization pass during encoding.

Scaling down tends to save the most, a GIF at 50% width and height is roughly a quarter the pixel data. Dropping frames helps most on animations with lots of near-duplicate frames. The compression level controls how carefully the encoder picks each frame's color palette, heavier settings encode faster and produce smaller files at some cost to color accuracy.

Common questions

What actually shrinks the file size?

Three levers, used together or separately: scaling the pixel dimensions down, dropping every Nth frame to reduce the total frame count, and a coarser color quantization pass during re-encoding. Scaling down usually has the biggest effect.

Will dropping frames make the animation choppy?

A little, since fewer frames means less smooth motion. The remaining frames' display time is stretched to keep the overall animation length roughly the same, so it plays at a similar speed, just with less in-between motion.

Is my GIF uploaded anywhere?

No, it's decoded, resized, and re-encoded entirely in your browser.
